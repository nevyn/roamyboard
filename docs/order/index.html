Build your Roamyboard

Pick a shape, bend it to fit your thigh, paint the keys whatever colours make you happy. This isn't a shop: the button at the bottom opens an email to me with your configuration in it, and we take it from there. Every price is what the parts actually cost from their vendor, line by line, so you can see exactly what you're paying for.

Curve

Each column meets the next at an angle, so a half hugs your leg. Thighs come in all shapes; pick the bend that fits yours. The electronics are identical either way.

Measuring your leg

Sit down (you were going to anyway). Lay something straight across the top of your thigh where the keyboard will rest, touching at both ends, and measure the span between those ends and the gap under the middle.

Fill both in and this will suggest a curve.

Switches

Kailh Choc v1, the low-profile switch the key module's hotswap sockets take. Hotswap means you can change your mind later without a soldering iron, as many times as you like.

Keycaps

Pick a colour, then paint keys with it. Go wild; nobody is grading this. Homing caps have a ridge you can feel; convex caps are dished the other way and suit thumbs. Caps come in packs, so the list below shows what you would actually buy.

Click a key on the keyboard to paint it. Arrow keys move between keys, space paints.

    The small print

    • This is a hobby project, not a shop. I build these one at a time, on evenings. Ask me how long it will take before you count on it.
    • Shipping is quoted separately, once you tell me where your legs live.
    • Parts prices are the vendors' own, read from their pages on the date shown under the bill, plus a small markup for the odds and ends no line item catches. They drift; the bill is an estimate until I order.
    • Five keys per column is what exists. Any other row count needs a new PCB, a new case and a new keymap, so it carries a design fee, quoted on request.
    • Assembly time is my own pace, soldering included: a quarter hour per key module, two hours per MCU module, half an hour per terminator.
